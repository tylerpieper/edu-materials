What Meshtastic Is

Meshtastic is free, open-source software for LoRa radios. It lets you send messages without cell service or internet.

Long Range

Handheld to handheld runs 1–3 miles in flat desert. A rooftop node with a decent antenna reaches 10–30 miles or more.

Decentralized

No dedicated router required. Every radio can relay traffic for the group, so the mesh has no single point of failure.

Encrypted

Messages are encrypted per channel. Only nodes holding the matching key can read them.

Long Battery Life

LoRa hardware uses very little power. Many nodes run for days or weeks on a charge, and solar nodes run indefinitely.

Optional GPS

Nodes can share position on the mesh so friends and family can see where you are. Position sharing can also be turned off.

No Phone Required

The radio keeps relaying and storing recent messages on its own. Pair a phone whenever you want to read or send.

Hop limit counts down

Each relay subtracts one before rebroadcasting. A node that receives a message with zero hops left does not repeat it. A hop limit of 3 carries a message across four radio links.

AZMSH suggests starting at 3 and staying at or under 5. Higher settings congest the channel.

Connecting in Five Steps

Budget about twenty minutes, most of it spent waiting on the firmware flash.

1

Update the firmware

Flash the latest build at flasher.meshtastic.org in Chrome. Back up your config first, because an update can wipe your private key.

2

Download the app

Install the official Meshtastic app from Google Play or the App Store.

3

Pair over Bluetooth

Power the radio on, open the app, tap plus, pick your device. Your node now appears in the list.

4

Get the AZ settings

Join the Discord and open the setup channel for the region's preset and frequency slot.

5

Say hello

Open Messages and post on the primary channel. Give it a few seconds, since mesh delivery is not instant.

Node Roles

Meshtastic's own guidance, and ours: nearly every node on the mesh should be Client, Client Mute or Client Base.

Anything mobile

Client Mute

Sends and receives, never rebroadcasts anyone else's traffic. Right for backpacks, vehicles and pocket nodes, and the safest role to start with.

Stationary home or roof

Client

The standard role. Rebroadcasts messages and extends coverage for your neighbors. Most stationary home nodes belong here.

Attic or roof base station

Client Base

Behaves like Client, but gives priority to rebroadcasting anything from or to its favorited nodes. Set your strongest high node to this, then favorite your other nodes on it.

Hilltops and towers only

Router / Router Late

Built for permanent, high-elevation, line-of-sight sites. In a house or on a mobile node it causes congestion and routing loops. Ask on Discord before switching.

The three client roles all rebroadcast sensibly and are safe to pick between. Router is the only one that can damage the mesh if it is used in the wrong place.

TAK Integration

Meshtastic bridges into the Team Awareness Kit used by search-and-rescue and outdoor coordination teams. It runs over the mesh with no cell service or internet.

1

Install the ATAK plugin

The official Meshtastic plugin for Android bridges Cursor on Target events between ATAK and your mesh.

2

Set the device role to TAK

This tunes the node for ATAK traffic and cuts routine broadcasts. Use TAK Tracker instead when the node has GPS but no ATAK device attached.

3

Chat and location flow both ways

Positions, messages and other CoT events move between ATAK and Meshtastic across the mesh.

Role: TAK

Optimized for ATAK. Needs the plugin running to send and receive chat, markers and other CoT events.

Role: TAK Tracker

Broadcasts position automatically and shows up on ATAK maps with no extra setup. Can be used as a standalone tracker.

A standard Client node does not participate in TAK. Only switch roles if you are actually running ATAK or WinTAK.

Testing Your Connection

Once everything is configured, check whether other nodes can hear you.

Say "test"

Send it on the primary MediumFast channel. Send it as many times as you like.

test
2 3 *

Tapback replies come back as numbers. The number is how many hops away the sender is, and an asterisk means they heard you directly.

Try a better location

Go outside, get higher (roof, balcony, second floor) and try at different times of day. Most problems at this stage are physical rather than technical.

Check your settings

A missing or wrong setting is the most common problem new operators hit. Start a thread in #i-need-help on Discord and someone will look.

Claim your node

Type /node claim in Discord, then react with the pie emoji in #getting-started to opt into diagnostics.

Read your traceroutes

Once opted in, view.azmsh.net/nodelist shows every hop your messages took and who traced you back.

Join the AZMSH community

Add the topic channels for traceroutes, hardware and help threads.
