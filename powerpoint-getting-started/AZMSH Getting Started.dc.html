What is Meshtastic?

Open-source, decentralized mesh networking over license-free LoRa radios.

Off-Grid Messaging

Share text messages, positions, map waypoints, telemetry, sensor data, and more — just not data intensive items like voice, pictures, or video. Does not rely on Wi-Fi, cell networks, or internet. If a message makes it from Nogales to Surprise, it did it over RF!

Decentralized Mesh

No routing tables or central servers required. Each message is handled independently and any node that hears it passes it along. No single points of failure and only minimal coordination required.

Encrypted

Unlike packet radio modes, Meshtastic is encrypted by default. Public channels use a simple shared key. Private channels can be created where only nodes with the key can read the messages. Direct Messages are encrypted using both nodes' keys. Even if your traffic is encrypted, the rest of the mesh will still relay your messages!

Why Use Meshtastic?

Bringing off-grid comms to the masses.

For Family & Friends

Because it operates under unlicensed Part 15 rules, you can hand a radio to your spouse, kids, or friends to keep in touch while camping, off-roading, or during a grid-down emergency.

More Than Messaging

Meshtastic has a rich position and telemetry feature set — track your bag, vehicles, or family. Attach a temperature sensor to a node and you can get environmental telemetry over the mesh. Other sensors include CO2, air quality, Radio Dosimeter, and more!

A Modern, Familiar Interface

For most nodes, all you need to do is pair it to your phone and keep it charged every few days. Messaging is done via an easy-to-use app on your phone. Just pick your channel, type your message, and hit send. Flip over to the map tab to see everyone's GPS location.

What About Ham Mode?

A powerful setting you will probably never use.

Part 97 Rules

Ham mode tells the firmware you are going to operate outside the Part 15 limits. This allows you to both operate at higher power within the 33cm ISM band as well as on other frequencies like 70cm.

No Encryption

The tradeoff is that you are no longer able to use any encryption, including the "barely encrypted" AQ== key used on the public channels. Enabling ham mode disables encryption in firmware, meaning direct messages are as public as channel messages — everyone can read everything.

A Separate Mesh

Due to encryption being disabled, a node in ham mode will not be able to communicate with nodes not in ham mode — ham mode is its own separate mesh. The vast majority of Meshtastic users are unlicensed — so very few hams use Meshtastic in ham mode.

Technical Overview

A look under the hood at the physical and routing layers.

Chirp Spread Spectrum

At the physical layer, LoRa encodes symbols as frequency-shifted chirps over time. A "chirp" sweeps across the bandwidth, allowing the receiver to decode signals even when they are deeply buried in the noise floor.

Flood Routing

When a radio captures a packet, it checks to see if it has heard that message before. If it hasn't, it rebroadcasts it. If it has, it drops it. This creates a resilient, self-healing, multi-path network.

Hop Limits

For each message a radio rebroadcasts, it marks the "hop limit" down by one. When a radio receives a packet with a hop limit of zero, it stops rebroadcasting. This strictly prevents infinite routing loops.

Radio Modem Settings

What goes into a preset and what we use in Arizona.

The Anatomy of a Preset

A modem preset defines the Frequency, Bandwidth, Spreading Factor, and Coding Rate. If any of these don't match, your node cannot hear other nodes. Meshtastic provides built-in presets like LongFast, MediumFast, and LongTurbo to make configuration easy.

Arizona Regional Settings

LongFast

The default preset for the United States. Since it's the default, we stay with it until the mesh density and traffic demands a faster preset.

MediumFast - Slot 18

Phoenix and Tucson switched to MediumFast a year ago to increase data rate. This preset should be used by anyone in, near, or between these metro areas since most of the mesh in Arizona is on MediumFast.

As the mesh expands, we are trying to get more areas connected to the MediumFast mesh.

Channels

How your node separates conversations.

Channel Hashes

The Channel Name and Encryption Key compute into a hash, which is stamped on each message. If a message is received with a channel hash that matches one of your configured channels, your node tries to decrypt it.

Encryption Keys

Public channels use a simple key that everyone knows. Private channels use a longer, highly secure key. Regardless of the channel, every message travels over the same radio mesh.

Node Roles

Meshtastic's own guidance, and ours: nearly every node on the mesh should be Client, Client Mute or Client Base.

Pocket and listen-only nodes

Client Mute

Sends and receives, never rebroadcasts. Use on any node that is not a good candidate to relay, like a pocket tag or an MQTT uplink in a closet.

Homes and Vehicles

Client

The standard role. Extends coverage for your neighbors. Most home nodes belong here. It's also great for vehicles, helping your pocket node get onto the mesh from inside a building.

Priority relay for your nodes

Client Base

Good for any node that you want to relay traffic from your own nodes (rooftops, vehicles, etc.). It gives priority to rebroadcasting anything from or to your favorites.

Hilltops and towers only

Router / Router Late

Relay every single message. Router will cut in line and rebroadcast before a Client gets a chance to. A poorly placed Router will degrade the mesh by cancelling other Client nodes. Router Late is useful to fill in gaps like those from terrain where a full router isn't needed.

The three client roles all have a place on your nodes and you can confidently use any of them that make sense for your application. Router Late and especially Router can actually cause problems for other mesh users if not applied correctly. If you aren't sure, ask in Discord!

Maximize Your Hardware

A few simple upgrades make a massive difference in range.

Placement & Elevation

Height is everything in radio. A cheap radio on a rooftop will easily outperform an expensive radio stuck inside on a desk. Get your node as high as safely possible.

Antenna Upgrades

Stock antennas are often terrible. Upgrading to a properly tuned 915 MHz antenna (like a Gizont or Alfa) is the cheapest way to instantly double your range.

Solar Power

Because LoRa is so efficient, a small 5W solar panel and a standard lithium cell can keep a roof node running indefinitely, entirely off the grid.

Connecting in Five Steps

Budget about twenty minutes, most of it spent waiting on the firmware flash.

1

Update the firmware

Flash the latest build at flasher.meshtastic.org in Chrome. (Note: if you are updating an existing node, back up your config first!)

2

Download the app

Install the official Meshtastic app from Google Play or the App Store.

3

Pair over Bluetooth

Power the radio on, open the app, tap plus, pick your device. Your node now appears in the list.

4

Get the AZ settings

Join the Discord and open the setup channel for the region's preset and frequency slot.

5

Say hello

Open Messages and post on the primary channel. Give it a few seconds, since mesh delivery is not instant.

MQTT and Diagnostics

AZMSH runs our own MQTT broker to power community maps and metrics dashboards. To participate, you must understand two distinct settings.

1. "OK to MQTT"

This simply gives permission for your node's basic diagnostic packets to be uploaded by someone else's MQTT uplink when they hear you. It is highly recommended to leave this ON.

2. Being an MQTT Uplink

If your node has WiFi, it can act as the bridge. It will upload public channel messages and all other packet types for both your node and any nodes you hear.

It does not process direct messages or your private channels. Even if you accidentally enable uplink for your private channel, our MQTT server can't decrypt the messages.

Testing Your Connection

Once everything is configured, check whether other nodes can hear you.

Say "test"

Send it on the primary MediumFast channel. Send it as many times as you like.

test
2 3 *

Tapback replies come back as numbers. The number is how many hops away the sender is, and an asterisk means they heard you directly.

Try a better location

Go outside, get higher (roof, balcony, second floor) and try at different times of day. Most problems at this stage are physical rather than technical.

Check your settings

A missing or wrong setting is the most common problem new operators hit. Start a thread in #i-need-help on Discord and someone will look.

Claim your node

Type /node claim in Discord, then react with the pie emoji in #getting-started to opt into diagnostics.

Read your traceroutes

Once opted in, view.azmsh.net/nodelist shows every hop your messages took and who traced you back.
